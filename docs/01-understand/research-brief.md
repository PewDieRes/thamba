## 3. Regulation and the zero-commission pilot

**Legal basis.** Motor Vehicles Act §2(1A) defines an aggregator by its function as a digital intermediary or marketplace connecting a passenger with a driver for transport. Section 93(1) requires a licence and also covers specified agents or canvassers who solicit customers for public service vehicles. It allows states to follow central guidelines when issuing licences and requires aggregators to comply with the Information Technology Act framework. Section 193(2) prescribes a ₹25,000–₹1 lakh fine for aggregation in contravention of §93 or its rules. [Motor Vehicles Act §§2, 93, 193][mv]

**Central and state framework.** MoRTH’s 1 July 2025 guidelines give states a licensing baseline, with a ₹5 lakh grant fee, five-year licence, driver and vehicle checks, safety and grievance provisions, and a fare range of 0.5–2 times the notified base with at least 80% to a driver onboarded with their vehicle. Maharashtra published its final rules on 15 July 2026 in Extraordinary Gazette No. 305, notification MVR-0821/C.R. 95/Tra-2. Use the Maharashtra requirements below for Malvan. The earlier transport department page still labels its 2025 document a draft. [MoRTH Guidelines, clauses 4–7, 8–18][morth] · [Final Maharashtra gazette][mh] · [2025 draft page][mh-draft]

**What a Maharashtra licence requires**

- **Applicant and approval.** Rule 7 lists a registered company, LLP or drivers’/vehicle owners’ cooperative registered under the Act named there (Co-operative Societies Act, 1912). It requires a Maharashtra office and sets conviction restrictions. Apply in Form I to the relevant RTA or STA. The form asks for registration and signatory details, proposed vehicle and permit particulars, GPS/GPRS and other infrastructure details, three years of returns and financial statements, and fee and guarantee details. Acceptance of a newer entity’s shorter financial history is **UNVERIFIED**. Confirm cooperative eligibility under the intended registration statute and whether proposed pickup and drop-off points fit the licence area and permits. The Sindhudurg Deputy RTO (MH-07) in Oras is the local starting point. [Rules 2, 4, 7 and Form I][mh] · [Transport department directory][rto]
- **Fees, timing and licence.** The grant fee is ₹2 lakh per district through an RTA or ₹10 lakh through the STA. The security is a bank guarantee of ₹10 lakh up to 1,000 non-bus motor vehicles. Rule 4 sets 90 days to decide the application and 30 days after fee and deposit payment to issue Form III. The initial term is three years. File the renewal paperwork at least 60 days before expiry. Rule 4 provides for five-year renewals on payment unless suspended or denied. Give 72 hours’ notice before launching, start within six months and display the Unique Licence Identification Number (ULIN) in the app and on the website. [Rules 2, 4–6, 8–9][mh]
- **Drivers and vehicles.** Budget for the 30-hour, five-day driver induction, identity and licence/PSV badge checks, police verification, medical and psychological fitness, five-year Maharashtra residency, a KYC bank account and written agreements in English and a language the driver understands, including Marathi under the rule. Verify permit scope, registration, fitness, insurance, pollution certificate, dues and e-challans. Autos must be at most nine years old at onboarding. Keep the prescribed driver and vehicle records. **UNVERIFIED** whether State Rule 13(1)(k) is applied to autos despite the three-wheeler exemption in the referenced central Rule 125H. Get the authority’s answer before budgeting for tracking hardware. [Rules 8–13][mh] · [MoRTH parliamentary answer on Rule 125H][vltd]
- **Operations and app.** Provide Marathi, Hindi and English, accessibility features, security certification by a CERT-In-empanelled firm, driver photo, trip sharing, fare disclosures, ratings, grievance officer and dedicated 24/7 grievance call centre. Keep the destination hidden from the driver until acceptance. Rule 14(11) bars differentiation between drivers or vehicles before allocation. Confirm how it applies to one stand’s rotation and how Rule 8(h), on admitting eligible permit holders, applies to stand membership. Rules also cover insurance choice, non-exclusivity, cancellations, electronic reporting and fleet directions. Queue and membership interpretation, and any currently applicable fleet targets, are **UNVERIFIED**. [Rules 8, 14, 16–20, 24–25, 28, 36][mh]
- **Fixed fares and zero commission.** Rule 16 bases fares on the RTA tariff and specifies a three-kilometre minimum base. Rule 2 sets the surge range at 0.75–1.5 times base fare, and Rule 16 guarantees the driver at least 80% of the fare. Rule 16(3)(a) phrases the upper bound differently, so confirm the applicable cap with the authority if using that model. The convenience-fee model caps each side’s fee at 5% of base, subject to STA revision, and guarantees drivers at least 95% of base. Form I lists these two models for this service. Whether the authority will approve a fixed RTA-derived fare, zero fees on both sides and 100% direct driver payment is **UNVERIFIED**. Ask for the current local fare order and written approval of the proposed model. Use drivers’ quotes as research input for the landmark fare sheet. The published Mumbai-region tariff is for MMRTA and does not establish the Sindhudurg rate. [Rules 2(1)(n), 2(1)(mm), 4 and 16 and Form I][mh] · [Sindhudurg office][rto] · [MMRTA tariff card][mmrta]

## 5. Rider and driver phone numbers under DPDP

**When duties start.** The Act’s definitions and institutional provisions began in November 2025. The Consent Manager tranche followed in November 2026. The core private-sector processing duties in §§3–17, apart from the separately commenced §6(9), and the main operational rules take effect 18 months from the printed Gazette date of 13 November 2025, giving **13 May 2027**. A phone number linked to an identifiable rider or driver is personal data. The body deciding why and how to use it is the Data Fiduciary, even if a vendor stores it. The Maharashtra licensing rules already require compliance with applicable IT and data law. [DPDP Act §§2–3, 8][dp-act] · [Commencement notice][dp-start] · [DPDP Rules][dp-rules] · [Corrigendum][dp-corr] · [Maharashtra Rules 7, 14][mh]

- **Purpose and lawful ground.** Specify each purpose for the number, such as booking confirmation, pickup coordination or driver account administration. Use clear affirmative consent where relying on consent and collect only data necessary for that purpose. Section 7(a) can cover a number voluntarily supplied for its specified purpose when the individual has not indicated objection. Assess that basis for the actual flow. Use a separate opt-in if adding promotional messages. Provide the consent notice in clear language, identify data and purposes, make it accessible in English or an Eighth Schedule language and describe withdrawal, rights and complaints to the Board. [Act §§4–7][dp-act] · [Rule 3][dp-rules]
- **Storage and sharing.** Restrict access, protect numbers with encryption or masking, log access, monitor and review it, and maintain recoverable backups. Use valid contracts with processors such as hosting, OTP or call-relay providers and include security and erasure obligations. The fiduciary remains responsible. Prefer masked trip contact where needed and keep raw numbers out of public rosters and routine analytics. [Act §8][dp-act] · [Rule 6][dp-rules]
- **Access and deletion.** Publish a privacy contact and a way to request access, correction, updating, erasure and grievance redressal or to nominate someone. Publish a reasonable grievance response period of at most 90 days under Rule 14. Make consent withdrawal comparably easy. Erase data when its purpose ends or consent is withdrawn, subject to mandatory retention, and have processors erase it too. Rules 6(1)(e) and 8(3) require one-year retention of specified personal data and logs for security and lawful purposes, with Rule 8(3) measured from processing. Map what must be retained, restrict access and erase after the applicable period unless a longer legal requirement applies. The Third Schedule’s inactivity periods apply to listed classes and thresholds. Thamba-specific exemptions or additional retention directions are **UNVERIFIED**. [Act §§6, 8, 11–15, 17][dp-act] · [Rules 6, 8–9, 14 and Third and Seventh Schedules][dp-rules]
- **Breaches and children.** Once the operational rules commence, notify affected individuals and the Board without delay after learning of a breach, with a detailed Board update within 72 hours unless extended. If taking bookings from under-18s, assess verifiable parental consent and applicable exceptions before collecting their numbers. An adult contact booking for the group is the simpler flow to test. [Act §§2, 8–9][dp-act] · [Rules 7, 10, 12][dp-rules] · [2026 clarification of §9][dp-order]
- **Current IT requirements.** The 2011 IT privacy rules require a covered body corporate to publish a privacy policy for personal information. A standalone phone number is outside Rule 3’s list of sensitive data. Driver bank and medical records fall within listed classes. The separate CERT-In directions require covered entities to report listed incidents within six hours and keep ICT logs for 180 days. [2011 IT privacy rules, Rules 3–4][spdi] · [CERT-In directions][cert] · [DPDP Act §44 and commencement][dp-start]

## Design implications for Thamba

1. **Separate discovery from operating a ride service.** For this stage, run stand interviews, count requests observed at existing stands and replay fictional trips on paper. Before live launch, get the authority’s written position on the operating entity, the licence area, fare model, rotation and site permissions. [§3 · Motor Vehicles Act][mv] · [Maharashtra Rules][mh]
2. **Measure whether a stand solves the problem visitors have.** Start with the Malvan bus stand and fort jetty. Observe Kudal and Sindhudurg after scheduled train arrivals and Chipi after a flight to compare unmet demand. Record requests, time to pickup, grouped passengers, luggage and onward mode without names or phone numbers. Ask who has used Yetav and record which alternatives actually supply a ride. [§1 · Local transport and Yetav][master] · [Yetav launch][yetav-malvan] · [§2 · Rail footfall][rail] · [Airport traffic][air-march]
3. **Use road-accessible landmarks.** Walk the shortlist with drivers. Agree on a Marathi and English landmark name, approach, entrance and precise place to wait. Include Hindi in a licensed app. Label the fort jetty and Devbag boarding point as separate from the boat destinations. Validate Kunkeshwar, station and airport service permissions before adding them. [§2 · Master plan][master] · [District attractions][district-tourism] · [§3 · Rules 3 and 14][mh]
4. **Write the rotation policy with drivers.** Simulate an auditable first-in, first-out queue and agree what happens on skips, timeouts, breaks, refusals, cancellations and walk-up jobs. Test the rules with the destination hidden until acceptance, and get the RTA’s view on eligibility and pickup zones. Keep queue priority independent of tips. [§3 · Rules 8, 14, 17][mh] · [§4 · Yatri Sathi gateway reporting][yatri-refusals] · [CCPA tipping order][ccpa]
5. **Work out one fare sheet and the cost of running the stand.** In interviews, document quotes, waiting, luggage and empty returns. Compare with the current signed RTA order when available. If the authority approves the model, show an approved quote before a request and give a receipt with the required breakdown. Budget separately for the bank guarantee, checks, training and grievance cover. [§1 · Local fare assessment][master] · [§3 · Rules 5–10 and 16][mh]
6. **Measure service reliability at a stand before expanding.** Compare a busy arrival window with quieter hours. After licensing, track requests, fulfilment, actual pickup wait, failed handoffs, fare disputes and trips per available driver per shift. Agree on a staffed pickup and complaints contact with each site. [§4 · Kerala fieldwork][kerala-field] · [Goa pickup disputes][goa-dispute] · [Mysuru driver survey][mysuru]
7. **Minimise phone-number collection at each stage.** Use anonymous counts and dummy numbers in tests. For an eventual live service, decide which contact uses actually require a phone number. Keep identifiable contacts in restricted storage, expose only what the trip requires and tie deletion to a documented retention schedule. [§5 · DPDP Act][dp-act] · [DPDP Rules][dp-rules]

## Open questions for driver interviews

- Walk through the last few tourist pickups. Where did the passenger find you, when did they arrive, where were they going and how long did they wait? How often do walk-ups, driver calls, stays and tour operators bring rides? [Local transport assessment][master] · [Local operator][local-taxi]
- Which tourist or driver has booked or accepted a real ride through Yetav or any of the six named apps recently? Which category, pickup, time and outcome did they see? What happened when a booking failed? [Yetav launch][yetav-malvan] · [Platform checks](#1-ride-hailing-coverage-and-how-tourists-get-around)
- Which train, flight and boat arrival windows matter most? What changes in April–May, monsoon, on weekends or during festivals? Which passengers need a larger vehicle or a second auto for luggage? [Konkan Railway][rail] · [Airport traffic][air-march] · [Tourism master plan][master]
- Where can an auto wait, pick up and turn around at the jetty, beaches, Rock Garden, stations and airport? Who controls each queue? Which landmark names do tourists recognise and where do people wait at the wrong entrance? [Master plan][master] · [District attractions][district-tourism] · [Goa pickup dispute][goa-pernem]
- How does the existing queue handle drivers returning from long trips, breaks, vehicle capacity, no-shows, advance requests and walk-ups? Who can review a disputed turn and what record would settle it? [Maharashtra Rules 8, 14, 17][mh] · [Yatri Sathi gateway reporting][yatri-refusals]
- What do you quote on the shortlisted routes? What matters for the empty return, luggage or wait time? Is there a current permit or authority fare sheet we can inspect? Which trips would you decline at a fixed fare, and why? [Local fare assessment][master] · [Maharashtra Rule 16][mh]
- Which vehicle and driver documents are current, which routes are within the permit, and what would make it hard to join a licensed stand? Who currently pays for stand operations, and who should vote on expenses and queue rules? [Maharashtra Rules 4–13][mh]
- Which phone, languages and connectivity do drivers have on shift? When do rider and driver need to speak directly, and who should see their numbers?
