# Malvan field interview guide

**Stage** UNDERSTAND · **Status** Interviews pending

Use this guide to test the assumptions in the [problem definition](../problem-definition.md#validate-in-interviews). Aim for 20–30 minutes per participant and speak in their preferred language where possible. Ask about a recent event before showing the fictional demo. Ask what happened in order and probe for counterexamples. Record reported behaviour, observations made during the interview and reactions to fictional scenarios as distinct entries in the note.

## Opening and consent

1. Say that this is research for Thamba, a contest demo that simulates auto bookings. Explain that the session involves questions about existing travel or stand practices and may include a fictional walkthrough. Malvan is shown as **Licence pending**. Keep the session to research and demonstration.
2. Explain what you will write down, who can access the anonymised notes and how the project team may use anonymised findings in the contest. Explain that taking part is voluntary and the participant can skip questions or stop at any time.
3. Ask for explicit verbal consent before taking notes and record only that consent was given. If the participant declines, end the interview. Ask again before starting an optional demo task. Use written notes only. Avoid audio, video and photos.
4. Give the participant their note ID. Explain that they may ask the interviewer to remove their note using that ID until findings have been combined into an anonymised summary. Explain how they can reach the interviewer without collecting their contact details.

## Drivers

- **D1, R2** Walk me through your last few tourist pickups. Where did each request come from, when did it arrive and how long did pickup take? How often do you get work through walk-ups, known callers, hosts and apps?
- **D1, C1** How was your most recent turn decided? What happened the last time there was a break, a skip, a timeout or refusal, a cancellation or a walk-up while a driver was waiting? Where did the returning driver rejoin, and who resolved a disputed turn?
- **D2** How did you decide what to quote and whether to take the last few trips? What did waiting, luggage or an empty return change? Is there a current fare sheet you can describe or show with personal details covered?
- **D1, D2** Use a fictional queue to show a normal turn and an exception. Ask who gets the next offer and why. Show an offer with pickup and a deadline, then reveal the destination and fare on acceptance. What would you want to know at each step? What should happen after a timeout, refusal or cancellation?
- **D3** What phone and data connection do you have on shift? Which languages do you prefer to speak and read? Ask the driver to explain a dummy offer in their own words and show how they would respond.
- Have you recently received or accepted a request through Yetav or another app in Malvan? Which app, what kind of vehicle and pickup, and what happened? When is direct contact necessary, and who should be able to see a phone number?

## Stand coordinators (union heads)

- **C1** Walk me through your last shift and the last disputed turn. How did you handle walk-ups, breaks, refusals, cancellations and returns? What record, if any, settled the decision?
- **C2, R2** Who sets queue rules, decides who can join and staffs the Stand? Which arrival windows are hardest to cover? Where can an auto wait and pick up, and who controls access at those points?
- **C2** Who supplies the current fare chart and who deals with the last-minute change of a quote, a fare complaint or a lost item? What would the Stand need to run a licensed service, and which decisions would need outside approval?
- **C1, C3** What phone, data connection, paper records and languages do you use? With a fictional queue log, show how you would explain a skipped turn and hand over to another Coordinator. Which terms or actions need explanation?
- **C2** What experience has the Stand had with Yetav or the other apps? Ask for a recent example and how the pickup ended. What approvals or documents would be difficult to obtain for a future licensed pilot? Record the type of document only.

## Homestay hosts

- **H1, H2** Tell me about the last guest who asked about an auto. What did they ask for, how did you explain the pickup and price, and what happened next?
- **H1, H2, R2** What did you try first? If that option was unavailable, what did you try next? How long did each step take? Ask for a case where a pickup went smoothly and one where it fell through, if they have both.
- **H1** Which meeting-point names or entrances were clear or confusing in recent examples? What happened if the guest got a different quote or waited somewhere else?
- **H3** What phone, connection and language did you use with the driver and guest? How would you pass on a fictional pickup card, and what would you change before sharing it?
- Has a guest or driver mentioned completing a local pickup through Yetav or another app recently? What did you see firsthand and what did someone else report?

## Tourists (Riders)

- **R1, R2** Think of your last attempt to get an auto locally. Where and when did you need it? What did you try first, what did you try next, and how long did it take to get picked up or choose another option?
- **R1, H1** What fare and pickup directions did you get, and from whom? When did you know the final fare? How did you recognise the meeting point, and did anything change?
- **R3** What phone, data connection and language did you use? Show where you would wait from a fictional pickup card and explain the price shown. Which words, names or cues helped you decide?
- Did you recently try Yetav or another app for a Malvan pickup? Ask for the app, approximate location, vehicle type and outcome. What did you try after any failed attempt?

## Anonymisation and note files

- Interview adults who consent. Use the participant's role and note ID in place of their name. Omit phone numbers, exact addresses, vehicle registrations, licence numbers, account identifiers, precise timestamps and other details that could identify them. Use broad time windows and public landmarks where useful. Generalise or omit identifying combinations of details.
- Keep anonymised notes in this directory. Use local, access-restricted working notes during collection if needed and delete those once the anonymised note is checked. Share only anonymised findings. Paraphrase accounts and use participant IDs when referring to evidence.
- Name files `<role>-<n>.md`, with a counter starting at `1` for each role. Use `driver-1.md`, `coordinator-1.md`, `host-1.md` or `rider-1.md` as the first filename for each role. The filename stem is the note ID. Create a note only after a consented interview.
- In each note, record the date, role, note ID, consent, a broad setting and interview language. Then document the recent example, current workaround, relevant assumption IDs, any demo task and teach-back, and open questions. For each assumption touched, record evidence for it, a counterexample or insufficient evidence. For app mentions, note whether the account is firsthand and distinguish a local pickup from an outstation arrival or a listing.
